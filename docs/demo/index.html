1 · Problem

Problem Statement

13.2

NASA model estimate of the equilibrium score at landing, against 74.9 before flight (eyes closed, sway-referenced support, 37 ISS crew)

20 of 22

eyes-closed trials with prescribed head movements on a sway-referenced support ended in a fall 2–5 h after Space Shuttle landing (11 first-time astronauts)

No ground team on the Moon or Mars

After an ISS return, a recovery team assists the crew as they leave the vehicle.

On a lunar or Martian surface, the crew must judge readiness without that team.

NASA lists the Risk of Altered Sensorimotor/Vestibular Function Impacting Critical Mission Tasks among the risks of human spaceflight.

Balance is most impaired at the moment of egress, and future crews will have to assess readiness without a ground team.

Wood et al. 2015, Table III p. A49  ·  Jain et al. 2010, pp. 627–628  ·  NASA Human Research Roadmap, risks list

2 · Approach

Model Overview

  1. 1

    Measure

    Three 20-s trials on a force plate in each of four conditions: firm floor and foam, eyes open and closed.

  2. 2

    Score

    The NASA equilibrium score of each trial, as a share of the crew member’s own preflight baseline.

  3. 3

    Decide

    Seven numbered rules return GO, CAUTION, NO-GO or CANNOT ASSESS and name the rule applied.

  4. 4

    Forecast

    After a valid test without a fall that is below GO, NASA crew recovery data give the retest time.

A standing-balance test becomes one of four results, with the rule that produced it and the recommended action.

Equilibrium score: Wood et al. 2015  ·  Baselines: HBEDB, 163 adults (Santos and Duarte 2016, PhysioNet)

3 · Safety

Decision Rules

1

An invalid recording yields CANNOT ASSESS.

2

A fall or a stopped trial yields NO-GO, whatever the score.

3

A score below 75% of baseline yields NO-GO.

4

A score below the healthy population floor (5th percentile) yields CAUTION and referral.

5

A score at or above the GO threshold, which allows for test–retest variability, yields GO.

6

Any other score yields CAUTION.

7

The least favourable condition sets the result. Overall GO requires two consecutive GO tests in separate sessions at least 2 h apart.

Source of each threshold

The 75% line follows the NASA working definition of recovery as a decrement of less than 25% (Jain et al. 2010).

The population floor and the test–retest allowance come from HBEDB adults without a reported disability and from published minimal detectable changes.

Behaviour under uncertainty

A fall cannot be masked by the median of the trials.

When the GO threshold cannot be reached, no retest time is given and the crew member is referred to the crew surgeon.

The rules are applied in a fixed order, and a fall, an invalid recording or a single GO session prevents an overall GO.

Jain et al. 2010, p. 630  ·  Santos and Duarte 2016  ·  Pang, Lam and Wong 2011, minimal detectable change

Retest Estimate

NASA crew recovery model

r(t) = 1 − D·e−t/τ for each condition, fitted to 37 ISS crew.

Time constants: 116.1, 99.4, 44.6 and 19.4 h.

The range comes from the published 95% limits of τ.

69 h: every condition GO, CAUTION until confirmed.

71 h: second GO session, overall GO.

Limitations

The reference group is adults without a reported disability, not astronauts, each recorded once. Other illnesses and medication are not excluded.

The recovery model describes return to Earth, and its transfer to the Moon or Mars is unvalidated.

Post-landing sessions are simulated.

The thresholds are provisional and not validated against egress outcomes.

Scores agree with NASA crew preflight values on the firm floor, and egress outcomes remain unvalidated.

6 · Summary

Summary and Next Steps

1

A standing-balance test is converted into an explicit, traceable egress decision for the crew surgeon.

2

NASA crew recovery data (Wood et al. 2015) set the retest estimate, and NASA OSDR Inspiration4 blood panels (OSD-569, OSD-575) give crew context.

3

The next steps are validation with repeated post-landing sessions, input from a portable force plate or wearable sensor, and vital-sign modules.
