The problem

Leaving the vehicle is the first task after landing, and balance is then at its worst.

Balance is impaired after a gravity transition

The vestibular system adapts to weightlessness. On return to gravity, balance with eyes closed falls to a fraction of its preflight level and recovers over days.

Readiness changes by the hour

A single test gives a provisional classification of balance at that moment. The crew surgeon also needs to know when a retest is worthwhile, and the egress decision remains a clinical judgement.

No ground team on the Moon or Mars

After an ISS return, a recovery team assists the crew. On a lunar or Martian surface, the crew must judge readiness themselves.

How it works

Measure, score, decide, forecast.

  1. 1

    Measure

    Three 20-s standing trials on a force plate in each of four sensory conditions: firm floor and foam, eyes open and closed.

  2. 2

    Score

    Each trial is converted into the NASA equilibrium score and expressed as a share of the crew member's own preflight baseline.

  3. 3

    Decide

    Seven numbered rules, applied in a fixed order, return GO, CAUTION, NO-GO or CANNOT ASSESS. Overall GO requires two GO sessions.

  4. 4

    Forecast

    If the result is not GO, the NASA crew recovery model gives the time at which every condition is expected to reach its GO threshold.
